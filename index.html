There is more to you than what needs fixing.

Most of us arrive carrying a list: what to change, what to heal, what to do better. Spiral Essence begins somewhere gentler. It assumes you are already whole, just a little crowded, a little tired, and a long way from your own quiet.

A spiral doesn't march toward a finish line. It circles back, each time from a slightly different place. That is how most real change happens: slowly, and by returning.

This practice is built on that idea. Reiki gives the body a chance to rest. Reflection gives the mind somewhere to set things down. Neither asks you to become someone else.

  • Rest is not a reward.

    You don't have to earn stillness first.

  • You set the pace.

    Every session follows what you need that day, and no more.

  • Nothing is performed.

    No jargon, no promises, no pressure to feel a certain way.

A quiet practice of coming home.

Reiki is a gentle Japanese practice of rest and attention. You stay fully clothed, seated or lying down, while the practitioner rests their hands lightly on or just above the body. There is very little to do, and nothing to get right.

Many people describe it as deep quiet: a slowing of the breath, a softening in the shoulders, a feeling of being met without being asked anything. Some feel a lot, some feel very little. Both are welcome.

Reiki is a complementary practice and is not a substitute for medical or mental health care.

The Spiral Path

10 September 2026 3 min read Molly Simon

Look closely at the natural world and you'll find the spiral everywhere. It's in the curl of a fern unfurling in spring, the chambers of a seashell, the seeds packed into a sunflower's face, the water circling a drain, and the great arms of galaxies turning slowly through space. Long before anyone wrote about energy or healing, people were carving spirals into stone. At Newgrange in Ireland, spirals cut into the entrance stone more than five thousand years ago still greet visitors today.

Why this shape, again and again, across cultures that never met? The spiral seems to express something we recognize intuitively: life moves in cycles, and yet it also moves forward.

That is the heart of the spiral path, and it's the reason we chose it as the symbol for Spiral Essence.

Returning, but never to the same place

Many people come to Reiki hoping for a straight line: a problem, a treatment, a solution. Healing rarely works that way. Old feelings resurface. Familiar patterns return. It can feel like going in circles, and that can be discouraging.

The spiral offers a gentler way to see it. When you walk a spiral, you do pass the same points again and again, but each time you're at a different distance from the center. The grief that returns a year later isn't the same grief. You meet it with more awareness, more tools, more compassion for yourself. You're circling, yes, but you're circling inward toward your own essence, or outward into a wider life.

The spiral in Reiki

Reiki practitioners will recognize the spiral from the first of the Reiki symbols, Cho Ku Rei, often called the power symbol, which coils around a central point. Many practitioners describe it as gathering and focusing energy, like drawing a scattered mind back to center. When we begin a session, we're doing much the same thing: inviting the body and mind to settle, slow down, and return to their natural balance.

You may also notice the spiral in how a session unfolds. Relaxation often comes in waves. The breath deepens, then catches, then deepens further. Thoughts drift in and circle away. There's no need to force anything. Reiki simply creates the space for your system to find its own rhythm.

Walking your own spiral

You don't need a treatment table to practice this. Try tracing a spiral with your finger, slowly, from the outside in, breathing with each turn. Or on a walk, notice the spirals around you in shells, vines, and eddies of wind. Let them remind you that growth doesn't have to be linear to be real.

Wherever you are on your path right now, you haven't gone backward. You're simply on another turn of the spiral.

Just for Today

27 August 2026 3 min read Molly Simon

In the early 1920s in Japan, a spiritual teacher named Mikao Usui founded the healing practice we now call Reiki. Along with the hands-on techniques he taught, he left his students something simpler and, many would say, more important: five short principles for living, known as the Reiki precepts, or Gokai.

Usui called them the secret art of inviting happiness and the spiritual medicine for many ills. He asked students to sit with their hands in prayer position (gassho) each morning and evening, and to repeat the precepts aloud and in their hearts.

Translations vary, but the precepts are commonly given like this:

  • Just for today, do not be angry.
  • Just for today, do not worry.
  • Just for today, be grateful.
  • Just for today, work honestly.
  • Just for today, be kind to every living thing.

Why “just for today” matters

The most important words may be the first three. “Never be angry again” is an impossible promise, and failing at it only brings guilt. “Just for today” is something we can actually hold. It brings us back to the present moment, the only place where change is possible.

This is the spiral again. Each morning we begin fresh, returning to the same five lines, but meeting them as a slightly different person than we were yesterday.

Do not be angry

This isn't a call to suppress anger or pretend it isn't there. Anger is information. The precept invites us not to be ruled by it, and to notice the heat rising, pause, and choose how we respond. In a Reiki session, we often notice how much tension the body stores around old frustrations. Letting it soften, even a little, can feel like setting down a heavy bag.

Do not worry

Worry pulls us into a future that hasn't happened yet. Reiki practice constantly draws us back into the body and the breath, into what is true right now. When you catch yourself spiraling into “what if,” try placing your hands on your heart or belly and simply feel their warmth.

Be grateful

Gratitude shifts where our attention rests. It doesn't deny difficulty; it simply widens the view so we can also see what's supporting us. Many practitioners end each day by naming three small things they're thankful for.

Work honestly

Sometimes translated as “work diligently on yourself,” this precept speaks to integrity: doing our work, whatever it is, with sincerity, and committing to our own inner growth. Healing is something we participate in, not something that simply happens to us.

Be kind to every living thing

The final precept widens the circle outward to other people, animals, and the earth. It includes ourselves, too. Many of us offer kindness to everyone but the person in the mirror.

A daily practice

Try Usui's own method for a week. Each morning and evening, sit quietly with your palms together, read the precepts slowly, and let them settle. Notice which line catches you. That's often the one with the most to teach you, just for today.

The Reiki Symbols

12 August 2026 3 min read Molly Simon

Few parts of Reiki spark as much curiosity as the symbols. Students first receive them in Level 2 (Okuden) training, and for generations they were kept private, passed only from teacher to student. Today they're easy to find online, yet many practitioners still treat them with quiet respect, a little like a family recipe that means more when it's handed down in person.

So what are they, and why do they matter?

Focus, not power

It helps to understand what the symbols are not. They aren't spells, and they don't generate energy on their own. Most teachers describe them as focusing tools: shapes and names that help the practitioner direct their intention and attention during a session. In Usui's own teaching, the symbols were training wheels of a kind, ways to help students feel particular qualities of energy until they could access those qualities naturally.

In Usui-based Reiki, three symbols are taught at the second level and a fourth at master level.

Cho Ku Rei the power symbol

Cho Ku Rei is usually drawn with a line across the top, a line down, and a spiral that winds around the central stroke. It's the symbol most closely tied to the spiral we love so much here at Spiral Essence. Practitioners use it to focus energy, to begin and seal a session, and to feel grounded and present. Some compare it to a light switch, a way of saying, “Here. Now. Let's begin.”

Sei He Ki the harmony symbol

Sei He Ki is associated with emotional and mental balance. Practitioners often use it when a client is carrying stress, anxiety, or old emotional patterns. If Cho Ku Rei gathers, Sei He Ki soothes. It pairs naturally with the precepts “do not be angry” and “do not worry,” as a gentle support for the mind and heart as they settle.

Hon Sha Ze Sho Nen the connection symbol

This is the most complex of the three, built from several Japanese characters. It's associated with connection across distance and time, and it's the symbol used in distance Reiki sessions. Many practitioners also use it when working with past experiences or sending healing intentions forward to a future event, such as a surgery, an exam, or a difficult conversation.

Dai Ko Myo the master symbol

Taught at master level, Dai Ko Myo is often translated as “great bright light.” It's associated with spiritual insight and with the Reiki attunement itself, the process a teacher uses to open a student to the practice.

The spiral connecting them all

Taken together, the symbols trace a path from the body to the heart to connection with others and finally to spirit, moving outward in widening rings. They mirror the precepts, too, which begin with our own anger and worry and end with kindness to every living thing.

In a session with us, you won't see the symbols drawn in the air in any dramatic way. You'll simply rest while they quietly guide the practitioner's intention. Reiki is a gentle, complementary practice that works alongside, not instead of, care from your doctor or therapist. Its gift is space, the space to rest, breathe, and return to your own essence.
